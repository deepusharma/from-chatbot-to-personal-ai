# Writing Guidelines

Voice and style rules for all three co-authors. Every piece — Track A and Track B — goes through the voice editor before publish.

---

## The Non-Negotiables

- **No em dashes.** Use a short sentence instead.
- **No rhetorical questions.** They are an AI writing habit and rarely land well.
- **Never open an article or LinkedIn post with "I."** Find a different entry point.
- **Never open with a compliment or scene-setter.** Get to the substance in the first sentence.
- **Specific over generic.** Every claim tied to a real data point or real observation. If you cannot point to a source, do not write the claim.
- **No filler superlatives.** "Robust", "powerful", "seamless", "elegant" — cut them.

---

## Voice

Professional but human. Confident without being arrogant. The achievements speak. Do not oversell.

We are practitioners writing for practitioners. The audience — CPOs, founders, VPs Engineering, CISOs — reads LinkedIn to learn something they do not already know. Every article has to earn the scroll-stop.

Active voice. Outcome-oriented framing. First-person where it is earned ("we ran this", "we found"), not performative ("we believe that AI will...").

---

## Sentence Structure

Vary sentence length. A mix of short and medium sentences reads more naturally than uniform structure.

Short sentences are fine. Not everything needs to be a complete formal sentence.

Read the output aloud. If it sounds like a press release, rewrite it. If it sounds like it was generated by an AI and cleaned up once, rewrite it.

---

## AI-Tell Avoidance

These patterns flag AI-generated or AI-polished content to the readers who matter:

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| Rhetorical questions | Delete. State the answer directly. |
| Em dashes mid-sentence | Split into two sentences. |
| "Worth noting that..." | Just note it. |
| "It is important to..." | Just say the thing. |
| "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..." | Cut the preamble entirely. |
| Three-part parallel lists ending with "and more" | Pick the two that actually matter. |
| Uniform paragraph length throughout | Vary it deliberately. |
| Every section ends with a summary sentence | Cut the summary. Trust the writing. |

---

## The Enterprise Reframe (Track A only)

Every Track A article carries one explicit beat that elevates the analysis beyond a hobbyist review. Label it "Enterprise reframe" in your draft so the voice editor can find it. It should answer: "What do builders of enterprise AI notice here that a hobbyist reviewer would miss?"

This beat is typically one paragraph. It does not need to be long. It needs to be specific.

---

## Track B Voice

Track B (setup posts) is allowed to be more technical and step-by-step. The audience is builders who want to replicate the work. But the AI-tell rules still apply. Write like a practitioner documenting what actually happened, not like a tutorial writer anticipating every edge case.

Specific over comprehensive. Document what you actually did and what surprised you. Skip the edge cases that did not come up.

---

## Citation Rule

Every specific number needs a source before it goes into a draft. If the number cannot be sourced, use a range or remove it. The Citation-Tables.md file tracks the status of every claim across all articles.

One wrong number on LinkedIn kills credibility with the audience that matters. Check before you write, not after.

---

## Co-Author Workflow

- One branch per article or setup post.
- Name branches clearly: `article-1-map`, `setup-a-openclaw`, etc.
- PR to main = ready for voice editor review.
- Voice editor reviews every PR before merge. This is not optional.
- Main branch is publication-ready at all times.

The voice editor's job is coherence, not control. The goal is one unified voice across five Track A articles — not five individual voices stitched together.

---

## What Good Looks Like

A good Track A article:
- Opens on the substance, not a warm-up.
- Has one clear argument, not three.
- Backs every claim with a citation or a specific observation.
- Has an enterprise reframe that a CPO or CISO would underline.
- Ends with a teaser that earns the next read, not a summary of what was just said.
- Passes the read-aloud test.

A good Track B setup post:
- Documents what was actually done, not what should theoretically be done.
- Notes what surprised, what failed, what was skipped and why.
- Links back to the corresponding Track A article.
- Is replicable by someone with similar hardware and experience.
